## Text 1 – About Us

About Us
CAVE WEAR
Rugged. Functional. Refined.

Cave Wear makes clothing for people who move between worlds: a trail in the morning, a city street by evening. Garments that feel right in the wilderness and look right at a bar or a family dinner.

### Why We Exist

Cave Wear started with a simple question: why should outdoor clothing look like outdoor clothing?

The idea came from my own experience. I've always felt most comfortable in functional, tactical clothing, but it doesn't always suit every occasion. I wanted something different: clothing that could handle the outdoors without looking out of place anywhere else.

My background in media technology, graphic design, and merchandise production shaped the way I approached the idea. Working with different materials and printing techniques taught me to pay attention to the details behind a garment, not just how it looks.

I wanted to bring that experience together with my love for nature, exploring materials that balance durability, comfort, design, and environmental responsibility. During my research, I came across wood-fiber fabrics, opening up new possibilities for what everyday outdoor-inspired clothing could be.

Cave Wear is built around three principles:

- **Built to Last.** A focus on durability and garments intended to be worn for years.
- **Made to Adapt.** Clothing designed to move between environments without compromising on function or style.
- **Quietly Confident.** No unnecessary noise. No oversized logos. Just considered design and details that matter.

### Our Symbol: The Cave Bear

The cave bear is an extinct species that once roamed prehistoric Europe. Its powerful build and adaptation to its environment made it a natural source of inspiration for Cave Wear.

But the symbol represents more than strength. It reflects the relationship between form and function: the idea that a design should have a purpose, and that every detail should contribute to the whole.

That philosophy is at the heart of Cave Wear. We want to create clothing that feels considered rather than complicated, functional rather than excessive, and adaptable rather than limited to a single setting.

The cave bear connects that philosophy to the natural world that inspires us.

### Just the Beginning

Cave Wear is still a concept in its early stages. The collection you see is a first look at what we envision building.

The direction is clear. The work is just beginning.

## Text 2 – Every Mark Has a Reason + Made to Stay (added 2 Nov 2026)

NOTE FOR THE ABOUT US: This text is only one or two sections of the About us,
not the whole page. It only covers the product system, how and why we use each
print method, and the environmental side. Everything in it is planned, not fact.

### SECTION 1: EVERY MARK HAS A REASON

Every Cave Wear piece starts with the same question: what does this garment have
to do, and what should the logo do on it? So there is no single method for every
piece. Each one gets the method that suits its fabric and its use. All of this
is what we plan for production.

The logo stays quiet: tone on tone, matte, a little lighter than the fabric.
You notice it up close. It doesn't shout.

EMBROIDERY
Planned for the hoodie and the half-zip. [Dardan: why embroidery here?]
On the half-zip, the bear sits on the diagonal yoke seam, so the seam runs through
the cut between its head and body. The construction becomes part of the logo.
The beanie carries the bear as an embroidered patch on the cuff, because a woven
label looked cheap.

PRINT
Planned for the Core Tee's chest. [Dardan: why print here?]
The neck labels are printed too and have no tag, because these pieces sit on bare
skin. They show only the size, the bear and cavewear.de, so people always know
where to get another one.

DIRECT-TO-GARMENT
Planned for the back print of the Supporter Collection. The ink should become part
of the fabric and not sit on top of it like a sticker. [Dardan: why this method
and not another one?]

SILICONE HEAT TRANSFER
Planned for the All-Weather Shell, because embroidery would pierce the waterproof
fabric. It is only slightly raised, so nothing can get pulled off. The Pass Gaiter
uses it too, so the wordmark can stretch with the fabric instead of cracking.

PRESSED IN
The bear on the Pass Gaiter's toggle is pressed in, not printed, so it can't wear off.

Sometimes the best decision is to leave something out. The Field Hoodie has no
back print: one embroidered logo is enough, a print adds cost, and nobody sees it
under a backpack.

### SECTION 2: MADE TO STAY

We don't make sustainability claims yet. The final materials and production aren't
chosen, and a claim needs evidence for the finished piece. What we can share is
how we plan to think about it.

The most useful thing a garment can do is stay in use. So the goal is simple:
essentials made for years, not seasons. Buy once, keep it. You only need one or
two pieces, and the collection grows one considered piece at a time.

That idea shapes the details:

- Construction: flatlock seams are planned on the T-shirt's shoulders, armholes
  and sides. The idea comes from military base layers, and the shoulders are where
  backpack straps sit.
- Colour: we plan to dye a little darker than the target colour. From my experience
  in print and production, colour fades with wear and washing. It should reach the
  target around wash 8 to 12, then stay right for a long time. This is experience,
  not a measured result. The first day looks a little less perfect, so the piece
  can look right for longer.
- Materials: we are exploring materials that balance comfort, durability, appearance
  and environmental impact. Wood-derived fibers are among the ideas. For the Pass
  Gaiter's toggle, recycled plastic is planned, tinted black all the way through.

Specific claims will only follow once the materials, the production and the evidence
are clear.
