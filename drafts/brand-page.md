## Text 1 – About Us

About Us
CAVE WEAR
Rugged. Functional. Refined.

We make clothing for people who move between worlds: a trail in the morning, a city street by evening. Garments that feel right in the wilderness and look right at a bar or a family dinner.

### Why We Exist

Cave Wear started with a simple question: why should outdoor clothing look like outdoor clothing?

It began on my first trip to Sweden, around midsummer 2022. I saw many different clothes, and they were mostly very practical. But outdoor clothing has a problem that goes through most brands that concentrate on functionality: the fit is a very basic fit that doesn't look very aesthetically pleasing on many people.

I've always felt most comfortable in functional, tactical clothing, but it doesn't always suit every occasion. And clothes that do look sharp have their own problems: either I spend a long time styling them until they look right, or I have to be careful in them all day. I love it when I can just live my life, put everything in the washing machine without thinking about it, and walk through thorns without worrying about ripping anything. So I wanted something different: clothing that could handle the outdoors without looking out of place anywhere else.

My background in media technology, graphic design, and merchandise production shaped the way I approached the idea. Working with different materials and printing techniques taught me to pay attention to the details behind a garment, not just how it looks.

I wanted to bring that experience together with my love for nature, exploring materials that balance durability, comfort, design, and environmental responsibility. During my research, I came across wood-fiber fabrics, opening up new possibilities for what everyday outdoor-inspired clothing could be.

Cave Wear is built around three principles:

- **Built to Last.** A focus on durability and garments intended to be worn for years.
- **Made to Adapt.** Clothing designed to move between environments without compromising on function or style.
- **Quietly Confident.** No unnecessary noise. No oversized logos. Just considered design and details that matter.

### Our Symbol: The Cave Bear

The cave bear is an extinct species that once roamed prehistoric Europe. Its powerful build and adaptation to its environment made it a natural source of inspiration for Cave Wear.

But the symbol represents more than strength. It reflects the relationship between form and function: the idea that a design should have a purpose, and that every detail should contribute to the whole.

That philosophy is at the heart of Cave Wear. We want to create clothing that feels considered rather than complicated, functional rather than excessive, and adaptable rather than limited to a single setting.

The cave bear connects that philosophy to the natural world that inspires us.

### Just the Beginning

Cave Wear is still a concept in its early stages. The collection you see is a first look at what we envision building.

The direction is clear. The work is just beginning.

So there is no single method for every piece. Each one gets the method that suits its

The logo stays quiet: tone on tone, matte, a little lighter than the fabric. You notice
it up close. It doesn't shout.

EMBROIDERY
Planned for the hoodie and the half-zip. On a fabric that can carry it, embroidery is
the best mark you can give a garment. From my experience, dyed thread keeps its colour
longer than most prints.

On the hoodie, the bear sits centred between the drawstrings. On the half-zip, it sits
on the diagonal yoke seam, so the seam runs through the cut between its head and body.
The construction becomes part of the logo. The beanie carries the bear as an embroidered
patch on the cuff, because a woven label looked cheap.

HEAT TRANSFER VINYL
Planned for the Core Tee's chest: a thin film pressed on with heat, like the numbers on
the back of a sports jersey. On a T-shirt, embroidery is a grey zone: it can work, but
the stitching on the inside sits against your skin. The Base Collection is worn all day,
every day, and should never feel noisy, so the Core Tee goes the safe way.

The Supporter Tee makes the other call. It's a statement piece, worn more in the city
than outside, so its logo is planned in embroidery to match the Supporter Hoodie, and
the two read as one collection.

DIRECT-TO-GARMENT
Planned for the back print of the Supporter Collection. The ink should become part of
the fabric and not sit on top of it like a sticker, so the piece falls better and feels
more natural. No screen is needed either, so each shirt size can get its own print size,
and the bear always stays in proportion.

I plan to use the system I worked with for four years, because I know how to set it up
so a print still looks good long after the first day. I have shirts I printed on it that
are worn out by now. The print still looks good.

SILICONE HEAT TRANSFER
Planned for the All-Weather Shell, because embroidery would pierce the waterproof fabric.
It is only slightly raised, so nothing can get pulled off. The Pass Gaiter uses it too:
it sits on your face, so the inside has to stay flat, and the wordmark can stretch instead
of cracking.

PRESSED IN
The bear on the Pass Gaiter's toggle is planned as pressed in, not printed, so it can't
wear off. There is no ink to fade and nothing to peel: the mark is part of the toggle
itself, for as long as the toggle lasts.

SOMETIMES THE BEST DECISION IS TO LEAVE SOMETHING OUT.
The Field Hoodie has no back print: one embroidered logo is enough, a print adds cost,
and nobody sees it under a backpack. The Supporter pieces are statement pieces, so there
the print is the point.

### SECTION 2: MADE TO STAY

We don't make sustainability claims yet. The final materials and production aren't
chosen, and a claim needs evidence for the finished piece. What we can share is
how we plan to think about it.

The most useful thing a garment can do is stay in use. So the goal is simple:
essentials made for years, not seasons. Buy once, keep it. You only need one or
two pieces, and the collection grows one considered piece at a time.

That idea shapes the details:

- Construction: flatlock seams are planned on the T-shirt's shoulders, armholes
  and sides. The idea comes from military base layers, and the shoulders are where
  backpack straps sit.
- Colour: we plan to dye a little darker than the target colour. From my experience
  in print and production, colour fades with wear and washing. It should reach the
  target around wash 8 to 12, then stay right for a long time. This is experience,
  not a measured result. The first day looks a little less perfect, so the piece
  can look right for longer.
- Materials: we are exploring materials that balance comfort, durability, appearance
  and environmental impact. Wood-derived fibers are among the ideas. For the Pass
  Gaiter's toggle, recycled plastic is planned, tinted black all the way through.

Specific claims will only follow once the materials, the production and the evidence
are clear.
